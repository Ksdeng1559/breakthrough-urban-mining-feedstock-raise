# Digital Fund Collateral Strategy

## Purpose

This document defines how Breakthrough Management can extend its currently paper-based, SEC-registered fundraising structure into a digital investor collateral and onboarding experience.

The objective is not to replace the legal fund documents. The objective is to build a digital layer around the existing fund structure that improves investor education, onboarding, diligence, tracking, reporting, and repeat capital formation.

---

# Strategic Thesis

Breakthrough Management currently has a paper-based fund process. A digital investor collateral layer can make the raise more scalable, more transparent, and easier for qualified investors to understand.

The model is inspired by customizable fund infrastructure such as Avestor's Customizable Fund model, where a sponsor can create a digital experience around fund participation, investor onboarding, document management, and deal-level transparency.

The legal foundation remains the existing SEC-registered or securities-counsel-approved structure.

The digital platform becomes the investor experience layer.

# Why This Matters

A paper-based raise is harder to scale because investor education, onboarding, document delivery, and follow-up must be managed manually.

A digital collateral system helps:

- reduce friction for investors
- improve professional presentation
- organize diligence materials
- support accreditation and onboarding
- track investor engagement
- enable repeat follow-up
- provide project-level transparency
- support future raises or deal sleeves

---

# Compliance Review Questions

Before deploying any digital fund platform, ask securities counsel:

1. Can the current fund structure be supported by a digital investor onboarding platform?
2. Can investors view different project sleeves without creating separate offerings?
3. What materials can be shown before accreditation verification?
4. What materials must be gated behind NDA or accredited investor verification?
5. Can investor allocation preferences be captured digitally?
6. What disclaimers are required on public pages?
7. What disclaimers are required inside the data room?
8. How should forward-looking statements and projections be displayed?
9. What audit trail must be retained for investor communications?
10. Does the digital process affect Reg D or SEC filing obligations?

---

# Platform Evaluation Questions

When evaluating Avestor or similar platforms, ask:

1. Can the platform support an existing paper-based fund structure?
2. Can it host investor documents and gated collateral?
3. Can it support accreditation verification?
4. Can it support multiple deal sleeves or project pages?
5. Can it track investor interest by sleeve?
6. Can it integrate with CRM or export investor activity?
7. Can it support e-signature workflows?
8. Can it support investor reporting?
9. Can it handle fund administration requirements?
10. Can it support future raises or additional funds?
