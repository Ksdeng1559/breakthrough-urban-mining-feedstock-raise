# Middle Market Definition and Positioning

## Purpose

This note defines how the Breakthrough Urban Mining Feedstock Raise should be positioned when speaking with capital brokers, private credit funds, family offices, strategic investors, and lower middle-market investment banking contacts.

---

## What Is Middle Market?

Middle market generally refers to companies that are too large to be considered small businesses but not large enough to be public-market, large-cap, or institutional mega-cap companies.

Common market categories:

| Category | Typical Annual Revenue | Typical EBITDA |
|---|---:|---:|
| Lower Middle Market | $5M–$50M | $1M–$10M |
| Core Middle Market | $50M–$500M | $10M–$50M |
| Upper Middle Market | $500M–$1B+ | $50M+ |

In private capital, investment banking, and debt/equity raise discussions, many institutions broadly define the middle market as companies with approximately:

- $10M–$1B in annual revenue, or
- $2M–$100M+ EBITDA

Definitions vary by lender, investment bank, broker-dealer, private equity firm, private credit fund, and strategic investor.

---

## Most Relevant Category for Urban Mining

For the Breakthrough Urban Mining Feedstock Raise, the most relevant category is:

## Lower Middle Market

This is the category where companies are often:

- Raising $2M–$25M
- Seeking growth capital, working capital, equipment financing, or project finance
- Commercially active but not yet large enough for major investment banks
- Asset-backed, infrastructure-oriented, or industrial in nature
- Attractive to boutique investment banks, capital brokers, private credit funds, family offices, strategic investors, and specialized financing groups

---

## Application to the $5M Urban Mining Feedstock Raise

The $5M raise should be positioned as a lower middle-market capital formation opportunity.

Recommended language:

> Breakthrough’s Urban Mining Feedstock Raise is a lower middle-market circular economy and critical minerals infrastructure financing opportunity focused on securing high-value electronic feedstock as the ore body of the digital age.

This framing is stronger than calling it:

- a startup raise
- a recycling business
- a speculative mining opportunity
- an ESG-only story
- a generic working-capital loan

---

## Why This Positioning Matters

The lower middle-market framing helps align the raise with capital sources that understand:

- industrial assets
- working capital needs
- feedstock acquisition
- inventory financing
- circular economy infrastructure
- strategic partner capital
- private credit
- family office direct investment
- specialized investment banking relationships

It also helps avoid mispositioning the opportunity as a venture software startup or a traditional mining exploration raise.
