# SBIR and Non-Dilutive Grant Strategy

## Purpose

This document maps potential SBIR/STTR and non-dilutive grant pathways for Urban Mining's R&D program.

The R&D thesis is to convert existing recycling facilities into AI- and robotics-enabled e-waste recovery facilities, fulfilling the circular economy aspect of the business model.

## Core R&D Thesis

Urban Mining is developing an applied R&D program focused on:

1. AI-assisted e-waste identification and classification
2. robotic sorting and disassembly of electronic waste
3. automation retrofit systems for existing recycling facilities
4. material stream optimization for PCB and server feedstock
5. circular economy reporting and traceability
6. recovery workflow intelligence for precious and critical metals

The grant narrative should not be framed as ordinary recycling. It should be framed as:

> AI- and robotics-enabled circular economy infrastructure for high-value e-waste recovery.

## Why This Matters

Existing recycling facilities are often labor-intensive, low-margin, and poorly optimized for high-value material recovery.

Urban Mining can use R&D to create a retrofit model that helps existing facilities:

- identify high-value e-waste streams
- separate PCB-rich material more accurately
- automate hazardous or repetitive sorting tasks
- improve recovery economics
- produce better chain-of-custody and ESG documentation
- increase circularity without building entirely new facilities

## Best-Fit SBIR Agencies

## 1. National Science Foundation - NSF America's Seed Fund

### Fit Level

Very High

### Why It Fits

NSF America's Seed Fund supports deep technology startups and small businesses across nearly all technology areas. Relevant topic areas include:

- Artificial Intelligence
- Robotics
- Environmental Technologies
- Advanced Manufacturing
- Natural Resources and Critical Minerals
- Ecomanufacturing
- Emission or Waste Reduction and the Circular Economy
- Digital Ecosystem for the Environment
- Instrumentation and Hardware Systems

### Best NSF Topic Positioning

Primary:

- Environmental Technologies / Emission or Waste Reduction and the Circular Economy
- Robotics / Robotic Applications
- Artificial Intelligence / Computer Vision Based AI Technologies
- Advanced Manufacturing / Ecomanufacturing
- Advanced Manufacturing / Natural Resources and Critical Minerals

### Suggested NSF Project Title

AI and Robotics Retrofit Platform for High-Value E-Waste Recovery in Existing Recycling Facilities

### Suggested NSF Phase I Objective

Develop and validate a prototype AI + robotic sorting workflow capable of identifying, classifying, and separating PCB-rich and server-derived e-waste streams in a recycling facility environment.

### Phase I R&D Questions

1. Can computer vision accurately identify high-value PCB/server components in mixed e-waste streams?
2. Can robotic sorting reduce labor intensity and improve material segregation quality?
3. Can the system be retrofitted into existing recycling facilities without a complete facility rebuild?
4. Can the workflow improve chain-of-custody and circular economy reporting?
5. Can the system create measurable increases in recoverable metal concentration?

### Phase I Deliverables

- computer vision model prototype
- robotic sorting proof-of-concept
- e-waste classification dataset
- facility retrofit workflow design
- economic model comparing manual vs automated sorting
- circularity reporting framework

## 2. EPA SBIR

### Fit Level

High, but timing-dependent

### Why It Fits

EPA SBIR supports environmental technologies that address EPA mission priorities and commercialization. Urban Mining's strongest EPA framing is pollution prevention, waste reduction, circular economy, and safer electronics recycling.

### Best EPA Positioning

- pollution prevention
- waste reduction
- circular economy
- hazardous waste minimization
- e-waste diversion
- environmental technology commercialization

### Suggested EPA Project Title

Automated Circular Economy Retrofit System for Safer and Higher-Value E-Waste Recycling

### Suggested EPA Phase I Objective

Demonstrate a technology pathway that helps existing recycling facilities reduce manual handling, improve separation of high-value e-waste streams, and increase diversion of PCB-rich materials from lower-value or improper disposal pathways.

### EPA Notes

EPA SBIR solicitations are annual and must be monitored. EPA's funding page should be checked for current open or upcoming solicitations before preparing an application.

## 3. DOE SBIR/STTR

### Fit Level

Medium to High, depending on topic release

### Why It Fits

DOE may be relevant where the project is framed around critical materials, advanced manufacturing, industrial efficiency, circular supply chains, or domestic materials security.

### Best DOE Positioning

- critical materials recovery
- advanced manufacturing retrofit systems
- AI-enabled industrial processing
- circular supply chains for electronics and critical minerals
- energy and materials efficiency in recycling operations

### Suggested DOE Project Title

AI-Enabled Industrial Retrofit System for Critical Material Recovery from Electronic Waste

### DOE Application Logic

DOE fit depends heavily on current topic releases. The strongest path is to monitor DOE SBIR/STTR topics for critical materials, advanced manufacturing, industrial decarbonization, and AI-enabled manufacturing.

## 4. NIST SBIR

### Fit Level

Medium

### Why It Fits

NIST may be relevant if the project focuses on measurement, standards, robotics, automation, traceability, or materials characterization.

### Best NIST Positioning

- measurement science for e-waste material streams
- AI-enabled material classification standards
- robotic automation for manufacturing/recycling systems
- traceability and chain-of-custody for recovered materials

## 5. DoD / AFWERX / Defense SBIR

### Fit Level

Selective

### Why It Fits

Defense relevance exists if the project is positioned around supply chain resilience, secure electronics disposal, critical material recovery, or domestic industrial base strengthening.

### Best DoD Positioning

- secure electronics destruction and recovery
- domestic critical materials supply chain
- AI-enabled material sorting for defense electronics
- robotics for hazardous material handling
- circularity in defense electronics supply chains

### Caution

DoD topics are mission-specific. Only pursue if an active topic clearly matches e-waste, critical materials, robotics, AI sorting, secure electronics lifecycle, or industrial base resilience.

## Recommended First Grant Concept

### Project Title

AI and Robotics Retrofit Platform for High-Value E-Waste Recovery

### One-Sentence Summary

Urban Mining is developing an AI- and robotics-enabled retrofit system that helps existing recycling facilities automate the identification, sorting, and recovery of high-value PCB and server-derived e-waste streams.

### Innovation Claim

The innovation is not simply recycling e-waste. The innovation is a modular retrofit platform that converts existing recycling facilities into higher-value, data-driven, automated recovery operations without requiring full facility replacement.

### Commercial Market

Potential customers and deployment partners include:

- e-waste recyclers
- ITAD firms
- data center decommissioning firms
- telecom asset recovery companies
- municipal recycling operators
- industrial recycling facilities

### Societal Impact

The technology supports:

- circular economy infrastructure
- waste reduction
- safer recycling operations
- improved recovery of precious and critical metals
- reduced need for new mining disturbance
- regional industrial modernization

## Strategic Conclusion

The non-dilutive capital strategy should support, not replace, the $5M raise.

The strongest combined message is:

> Investor capital secures feedstock. Non-dilutive grant capital funds the R&D automation layer that increases recovery efficiency and converts existing recycling facilities into circular economy infrastructure.

This creates a more powerful capital stack:

- private capital for supply control
- grants for R&D
- strategic partnerships for facility deployment
- future revenue from processing and technology licensing
