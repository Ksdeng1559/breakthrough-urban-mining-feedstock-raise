# NSF Project Pitch Draft

## Working Title

AI and Robotics Retrofit Platform for High-Value E-Waste Recovery

## Applicant Concept

Urban Mining is developing an AI- and robotics-enabled retrofit platform that helps existing recycling facilities identify, sort, and recover high-value e-waste streams, including printed circuit boards, retired servers, telecom hardware, and enterprise electronics.

The platform is intended to improve the economics, safety, and circularity of e-waste recycling by converting existing manual or semi-manual recycling operations into intelligent recovery facilities.

---

# 1. Technology Innovation

## Problem

Existing e-waste recycling facilities often rely on manual sorting, inconsistent material classification, limited automation, and low-resolution tracking of material streams.

As a result:

- PCB-rich material may be misclassified or under-valued
- high-value server and telecom components may be mixed into lower-value streams
- sorting labor can be repetitive, hazardous, and difficult to scale
- chain-of-custody data may be incomplete
- circular economy reporting may be weak
- recovery economics may be limited by poor input segregation

## Proposed Innovation

Urban Mining proposes to develop a modular AI and robotics retrofit system for existing recycling facilities.

The system will combine:

1. computer vision for e-waste component recognition
2. AI classification of PCB-rich and server-derived material streams
3. robotic sorting or assisted picking workflows
4. material stream scoring for recovery value
5. chain-of-custody data capture
6. circular economy reporting outputs

## Technical Novelty

The innovation is not simply recycling e-waste.

The innovation is a retrofit platform that helps existing recycling facilities become higher-value, data-driven, automated urban mining operations without requiring full facility replacement.

---

# 2. Technical Objectives

## Phase I Objective

Develop and validate a proof-of-concept AI and robotics workflow capable of identifying, classifying, and separating high-value PCB and retired server components from mixed e-waste streams in a recycling facility context.

## Key Technical Questions

1. Can computer vision reliably identify PCB-rich and server-derived components in mixed e-waste streams?
2. Can an AI model classify material by likely recovery value or processing priority?
3. Can robotic or semi-robotic sorting improve separation quality compared with manual sorting?
4. Can the system be designed as a retrofit for existing recycling facilities?
5. Can material stream data be captured for chain-of-custody and circularity reporting?

## Phase I Technical Deliverables

- e-waste image dataset specification
- prototype computer vision model
- material classification taxonomy
- robotic sorting workflow design
- retrofit architecture diagram
- pilot facility requirements document
- techno-economic comparison of manual vs. AI-assisted sorting
- circularity reporting proof-of-concept

# 10. Suggested NSF Project Pitch Summary

Urban Mining is developing a modular AI and robotics retrofit platform that enables existing recycling facilities to identify, sort, and route high-value e-waste streams such as printed circuit boards, retired servers, and telecom hardware. Current recycling operations often rely on manual sorting and inconsistent material classification, causing valuable PCB-rich material to be under-recovered or misallocated. The proposed system combines computer vision, AI material classification, robotic or semi-robotic sorting workflows, and chain-of-custody reporting to improve recovery economics and circularity. Phase I will validate whether AI-assisted identification and sorting can improve the separation of high-value e-waste streams in an existing facility environment. If successful, the platform can be commercialized as a retrofit system for e-waste recyclers, ITAD firms, data center decommissioning providers, telecom recovery companies, and municipal recycling operators.
