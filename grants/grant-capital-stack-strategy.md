# Grant and Capital Stack Strategy

## Purpose

This document defines the blended capital stack for the Urban Mining feedstock acquisition and R&D automation strategy.

The goal is to avoid relying on a single source of funding. The business should combine private capital, non-dilutive grants, strategic partnerships, and future technology financing.

---

# Capital Stack Overview

## Layer 1 - Private Capital

### Purpose

Secure PCB, retired server, telecom, and high-value e-waste feedstock.

### Target Raise

$5,000,000

### Use of Funds

- feedstock acquisition
- inventory financing
- logistics
- working capital
- supplier onboarding
- business development

### Strategic Role

Private capital controls the input stream.

In urban mining, feedstock is the ore body.

---

## Layer 2 - Non-Dilutive Grant Capital

### Purpose

Fund R&D for AI, robotics, automation, and facility retrofit systems.

### Target Programs

- NSF SBIR/STTR
- EPA SBIR
- DOE SBIR/STTR
- NIST SBIR
- DoD / AFWERX selective topics
- state and provincial circular economy grants
- economic development grants
- workforce development grants

### Strategic Role

Grant capital should fund the technology layer, not the feedstock acquisition layer.

Investor capital secures supply. Grant capital improves how the supply is processed, sorted, tracked, and monetized.

---

## Layer 3 - Strategic Partner Capital

### Purpose

Secure partnerships with companies that can provide feedstock, facilities, logistics, or downstream processing support.

### Potential Strategic Partners

- ITAD firms
- e-waste recyclers
- data center decommissioning companies
- telecom asset recovery companies
- industrial recyclers
- precious metals ecosystem participants
- municipal recycling operators

### Strategic Role

Strategic partners can contribute:

- feedstock
- pilot facilities
- operational expertise
- logistics support
- customer validation
- capital
- letters of support for grant applications

---

## Layer 4 - Technology Financing

### Purpose

Fund the future Urban Mining technology platform if AI/robotics R&D validates.

### Potential Future Financing

- venture capital
- strategic technology investors
- robotics investors
- circular economy funds
- climate technology funds
- licensing partners

### Strategic Role

This layer should only be pursued after evidence exists that the AI/robotics retrofit platform can become a scalable technology product.

---

# Recommended Capital Sequence

## Step 1 - Build Feedstock Proof

Actions:

- identify supplier accounts
- secure LOIs
- validate material streams
- create feedstock pipeline dashboard

Funding Source:

- internal resources
- initial private capital

## Step 2 - Raise Initial Private Capital

Actions:

- raise first $500K to $1.5M
- acquire initial feedstock
- demonstrate commercial traction

Funding Source:

- family offices
- strategic investors
- resource investors

## Step 3 - Apply for Non-Dilutive R&D Funding

Actions:

- submit NSF Project Pitch
- prepare EPA / DOE variants
- secure pilot facility letters
- identify technical partners

Funding Source:

- SBIR/STTR
- circular economy grants
- economic development grants

## Step 4 - Use Grants to Build Technology Layer

Actions:

- develop AI vision model
- design robotics workflow
- validate retrofit architecture
- create material classification system
- build circularity reporting prototype

Funding Source:

- non-dilutive grants

## Step 5 - Expand Strategic Partnerships

Actions:

- convert supplier relationships into pilot deployments
- pursue co-development agreements
- develop facility modernization package

Funding Source:

- strategic partners
- pilot customers
- matching grants where applicable

## Step 6 - Consider Technology Growth Capital

Actions:

- spin out or separate technology platform if justified
- pursue robotics / AI / climate tech investors
- build licensing model

Funding Source:

- venture or strategic technology capital

# Best Use of Each Capital Type

| Capital Type | Best Use | Avoid Using For |
|---|---|---|
| Private Capital | Feedstock, inventory, working capital | speculative R&D without proof |
| SBIR / Grants | AI, robotics, automation, retrofit R&D | ordinary operating expenses |
| Strategic Capital | pilot facilities, supplier access, market validation | unfocused expansion |
| Venture Capital | scalable technology platform | commodity feedstock purchasing |
| Debt / Inventory Finance | working capital after repeatable economics | pre-revenue uncertainty |

# Grant Positioning

The strongest grant narrative is:

> AI- and robotics-enabled circular economy infrastructure for high-value e-waste recovery.

The project should be framed around:

- facility modernization
- automation retrofit systems
- safer recycling operations
- higher-value material recovery
- circular economy reporting
- domestic resource recovery
- reduced dependence on new mining disturbance

---

# Investor Positioning

The strongest investor narrative is:

> Private capital secures the feedstock. Grant capital funds the automation layer. Strategic partners validate deployment. Together they create a scalable Urban Mining platform.

This helps investors understand that their capital is not being diluted across unfocused R&D.

# Strategic Conclusion

The Urban Mining opportunity should be capitalized using a blended funding strategy.

Private capital should secure supply.

Grant capital should fund R&D.

Strategic partners should validate deployment.

Future technology capital should scale the automation platform only after proof exists.

This sequencing protects investor confidence while creating a stronger long-term Urban Mining platform.
