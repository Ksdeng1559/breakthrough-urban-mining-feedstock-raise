# Enterprise Account Mega Deal Playbook

## Purpose

This playbook translates enterprise account selling principles into the Breakthrough Urban Mining Feedstock Raise.

The goal is not to sell recycling services.

The goal is to create strategic enterprise relationships that secure feedstock, capital, partnerships, grants, and long-term operating leverage for the Urban Mining platform.

---

## Core Enterprise Thesis

In conventional mining, reserves determine value.

In urban mining, secured feedstock determines value.

Feedstock is the ore body of the digital age.

Breakthrough is raising capital to secure high-value printed circuit board, retired server, telecom, and enterprise electronics feedstock before the market fully reprices PCB-rich and server-derived material.

The enterprise account strategy is designed to identify, engage, qualify, and convert large organizations that can materially accelerate feedstock control, investor credibility, and strategic market position.

## Multi-Threading Model

Enterprise accounts should not be handled through one contact.

Each priority account should include at least three mapped stakeholders.

| Stakeholder Type | Role | Primary Concern | Desired Conversion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economic Buyer | CFO, CEO, VP, owner, principal | financial value, risk, strategic upside | meeting, LOI, investment, supply agreement |
| Technical Evaluator | operations, facilities, sustainability, IT asset lead | feasibility, logistics, compliance | material review, qualification, pilot |
| Internal Champion | manager, advisor, analyst, operator | making the initiative succeed internally | referral, internal advocacy, next meeting |
| Legal / Compliance | counsel, risk, procurement | liability, documentation, chain of custody | approved process path |
| Finance / Investor Lead | principal, CIO, investment analyst | return profile, diligence, capital protection | data room access, soft circle |

## Account Qualification Scorecard

Each target account should be scored before outreach.

| Category | Score 1 | Score 3 | Score 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Feedstock Potential | unknown or small | moderate recurring material | large recurring PCB/server/telecom volume |
| Decision Access | no contact path | indirect contact | clear executive or champion path |
| Strategic Fit | generic recycling fit | useful supplier or partner | platform-level strategic value |
| Timing Signal | no trigger | possible upgrade or refresh | active closure, refresh, disposition, or procurement event |
| Economic Value | low | moderate | material impact on raise or feedstock control |
| Relationship Leverage | cold | weak second-degree path | warm introduction or trusted relationship |

Priority scoring:

- 24–30: Tier 1 strategic account
- 18–23: Tier 2 qualified target
- 12–17: nurture or research further
- below 12: deprioritize

## Discovery Call Framework

### Opening Frame

We are not approaching this as a recycling transaction. We are mapping high-value urban mining feedstock streams that may support a larger critical-materials recovery platform.

### Feedstock Discovery Questions

- What types of retired electronics, servers, telecom equipment, or circuit boards does your organization generate?
- How often does this material become available?
- Who currently handles disposition, recycling, or resale?
- Is the material separated, sorted, mixed, or processed before pickup?
- Are there chain-of-custody, data destruction, environmental, or compliance requirements?
- Are there upcoming refresh, decommissioning, relocation, or consolidation events?
- Would your organization consider a qualified review of material value and recovery potential?

### Strategic Partner Questions

- Where do you currently see bottlenecks in feedstock supply?
- What material types are most valuable or hardest to secure?
- Are you looking for new upstream supply relationships?
- What technical validation would be required before a pilot?
- Would a feedstock-backed financing model be valuable to your organization?

### Investor Questions

- Do you currently evaluate critical minerals, circular economy, infrastructure, or resource recovery opportunities?
- How do you view feedstock control as a risk-reduction mechanism?
- What proof points would be required before deeper diligence?
- Would signed feedstock LOIs improve your willingness to evaluate the opportunity?
- What capital structure would best fit your mandate?

## Champion Development Strategy

The internal champion is the most important person in the enterprise account.

A champion should have:

- access to decision-makers
- a reason to care about the outcome
- ability to explain the opportunity internally
- willingness to introduce additional stakeholders
- enough trust to share timing, process, and objections

Champion enablement assets:

- one-page strategic summary
- short video explainer
- feedstock value memo
- risk and compliance FAQ
- proposed next-step email
- LOI template
- data room access path

## 90-Day Enterprise Account Sprint

### Days 1–15: Account Universe Build

- define Tier 1 feedstock accounts
- define Tier 1 investor accounts
- define Tier 1 strategic partner accounts
- enrich company and stakeholder data
- score accounts using the qualification scorecard

### Days 16–30: Outreach and Champion Mapping

- launch first outreach sequences
- send supplier and investor explainers
- identify internal champions
- book first discovery calls
- record objections and signals

### Days 31–60: Discovery and LOI Development

- complete feedstock discovery calls
- complete investor discovery calls
- qualify material value and volume
- move serious accounts toward LOI or data room
- use Loom follow-up after every serious conversation

### Days 61–90: Conversion and Investor Proof

- secure first supplier LOIs
- prepare feedstock proof summary
- update investor one-pager
- open data room for qualified investors
- review pipeline health
- decide whether to scale, refine, or reposition

## Operating Principle

Do not chase every possible supplier or investor.

Prioritize accounts that can materially change the value of the platform.

The objective is not activity.

The objective is strategic control of feedstock, capital, and relationships.
