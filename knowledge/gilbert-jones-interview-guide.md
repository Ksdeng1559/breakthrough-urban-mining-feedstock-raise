# Gilbert Jones Interview Guide

## Purpose

Capture Gilbert Jones' feedstock expertise so it can be converted into reusable RIOS/OpenClaw worker instructions.

The goal is to extract the practical decision rules that Gilbert uses to identify, evaluate, price, and avoid feedstock opportunities.

---

## Module 1: Feedstock Fundamentals

1. What does the term feedstock mean in this business?
2. What are the main categories of electronic feedstock?
3. Which feedstock categories are most valuable?
4. Which feedstock categories should be avoided?
5. What terms do sellers use instead of feedstock?
6. What makes one lot better than another lot?

---

## Module 2: Feedstock Sources

1. Where does the best feedstock usually originate?
2. Which industries generate the highest-value material?
3. Which organizations are most likely to produce recurring feedstock?
4. What events create availability?
5. Who usually controls disposal decisions?
6. What relationships matter most?

---

## Module 3: Recovery Economics

1. How do you estimate value before buying material?
2. What gold, silver, copper, and other metal assumptions are used by feedstock category?
3. What purchase price per tonne is acceptable by category?
4. What freight and logistics costs must be included?
5. What processing costs must be included?
6. What margin makes a deal worthwhile?
7. What margin is too thin?

---

## Module 4: Processing and Risk

1. What happens after feedstock is acquired?
2. What costs are usually underestimated?
3. What contamination issues matter?
4. What paperwork or compliance issues matter?
5. What feedstock looks valuable but is not?
6. What deal structures create risk?

---

## Module 5: Buyer and Refiner Intelligence

1. Who buys recovered metals or processed outputs?
2. What refiners or downstream processors matter?
3. What terms do buyers require?
4. How is payment usually structured?
5. What quality or assay documentation is required?

---

## Module 6: Deal Red Flags

1. What sellers should be avoided?
2. What material should be rejected immediately?
3. What questions reveal a bad deal?
4. What hidden costs destroy margin?
5. What signs suggest the seller does not control the material?

---

## Interview Output

The interview should produce:

- Feedstock ontology
- Source map
- Recovery economics assumptions
- Pricing rules
- Risk rules
- Relationship map
- First worker training examples
