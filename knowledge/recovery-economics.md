# Recovery Economics

## Purpose

This document defines the first-pass economic model for evaluating electronic feedstock opportunities.

The model is intended for screening and human review only. It should not be used to approve purchases without expert validation from Gilbert Jones or an authorized MineTeck operator.

---

## Core Formula

```text
Total Cost = Purchase Cost + Freight Cost + Labour Cost + Processing Cost + Assay/Refining Cost + Compliance Cost

Estimated Recovery Value = Gold Value + Silver Value + Copper Value + Other Recoverable Value

Estimated Gross Margin = Estimated Recovery Value - Total Cost
```

---

## Required Inputs

### Feedstock Description

- Feedstock type
- Source organization
- Estimated weight in tonnes
- Known equipment category
- Known manufacturer or generation, if available
- Photos or inventory list, if available

### Acquisition Costs

- Purchase price per tonne
- Total purchase price
- Required deposit
- Revenue share terms, if applicable

### Logistics Costs

- Pickup location
- Distance to processing site
- Loading requirements
- Freight cost estimate
- Storage requirements

### Processing Costs

- Labour
- Dismantling
- Sorting
- Assay
- Refining
- Waste disposal
- Compliance handling

### Recovery Assumptions

- Gold grams per tonne
- Silver grams per tonne
- Copper kilograms per tonne
- Other recoverable materials

---

## Acquisition Screen

A feedstock opportunity should be evaluated using the following questions:

1. What is the likely feedstock category?
2. What is the estimated grade?
3. What is the estimated weight?
4. What is the acquisition cost?
5. What are the freight and handling costs?
6. What are the processing costs?
7. What metals or materials are recoverable?
8. What is the estimated gross margin?
9. What is the confidence level?
10. What expert review is required?

---

## Risk Notes

Recovery assumptions can vary significantly by equipment generation, manufacturer, age, source, contamination, and sorting quality.

Do not rely on generic e-waste averages for purchase decisions.

Actual lot results should be stored and used to improve future assumptions.
